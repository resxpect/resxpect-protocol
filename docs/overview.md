# RESXPECT Protocol Overview

RESXPECT is an agreement protocol for creating a verifiable record around work, evidence, outcomes and reputation.

The protocol is designed around a simple principle:

> Reputation should be based on what someone can prove they have done, not simply what they claim they can do.

## The Agreement

Every RESXPECT interaction begins with an agreement between two parties:

- **Creator** — creates or funds the agreement.
- **Runner** — performs the agreed work.

The agreement establishes what is expected before work begins and provides the reference point for evidence, review and resolution.

## Core Flow

A typical RESXPECT agreement follows:

**Agreement → Funding → Start Work → Evidence → Review → Completion**

Successful completion can contribute to a participant's reputation.

The agreement and the evidence behind it remain connected to that reputation.

## Evidence

Evidence provides a record of what happened during an agreement.

Depending on the work being performed, evidence may include information such as written submissions, files, images or other verifiable outputs.

Evidence is used during normal agreement review and may also become relevant during validation or dispute resolution.

## Reputation

RESXPECT reputation is designed to emerge from completed agreements rather than standalone ratings or self-declared claims.

A completed agreement can contribute to a participant's reputation, subject to the rules and validation mechanisms of the protocol.

Reputation is therefore connected to an underlying record:

**Agreement + Work + Evidence + Outcome**

## Validators

Validators provide an independent layer of review across the protocol.

Their responsibilities are divided into three primary areas:

### Precheck Validation

Provides additional review when an agreement requires assessment before work proceeds.

### Reputation Validation

Checks whether reputation being generated is supported by the agreement, work and evidence behind it.

### Dispute Resolution

Provides neutral resolution when the parties to an agreement cannot reach the same conclusion about the outcome.

## Protocol Transparency

This repository documents the public rules and intended behaviour of RESXPECT.

It does not contain the production backend, private infrastructure, security-sensitive systems or internal implementation details.

Public specifications may evolve as the protocol is tested and developed. Significant changes will be recorded through Git history and the protocol changelog.
