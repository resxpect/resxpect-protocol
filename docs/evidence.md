# Evidence

Evidence is the record that connects an agreement to the work that was actually performed.

RESXPECT uses evidence to help make completed work reviewable and to support reputation, validation and dispute resolution.

## Why Evidence Matters

A claim that work was completed is not enough on its own.

Evidence provides something that can be reviewed against the original agreement.

The relationship is:

**Agreement → Work → Evidence → Outcome**

This allows reputation to remain connected to something that can be examined rather than relying only on ratings or statements between participants.

## Evidence Types

The type of evidence required depends on the work being performed.

Evidence may include:

- Written submissions
- Images
- Files
- Documents
- Source files
- Inspection material
- Verifiable outputs
- Other relevant proof of completion

Not every agreement requires the same type of evidence.

The evidence should be appropriate to the work and the outcome being claimed.

## Evidence and Review

When work is submitted, the Creator can review the evidence alongside the original agreement.

The purpose is to determine whether the agreed work has been completed.

If the delivery is accepted, the agreement can proceed toward completion.

If the outcome is contested, the evidence can become part of the record used during dispute resolution.

## Evidence and Reputation

Reputation generated through RESXPECT is intended to remain connected to the agreements and evidence behind it.

This means reputation represents a history of completed, reviewable work rather than an isolated score.

## Evidence and Validation

Evidence may also be reviewed during Reputation Validation.

Validators may assess whether evidence is relevant, valid and consistent with the agreement being used to generate reputation.

## Public Specification

This document describes the public evidence principles used by RESXPECT.
