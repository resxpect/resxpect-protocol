# Reputation

RESXPECT reputation is designed to reflect completed, verifiable activity rather than self-declared ability or isolated ratings.

The aim is to connect reputation to a record that can be reviewed.

## Core Principle

Reputation should not be built on what someone claims they can do.

It should be built on what they can prove they have done.

Within RESXPECT, reputation is therefore connected to:

**Agreement → Work → Evidence → Outcome → Reputation**

## Reputation from Agreements

Qualifying completed agreements can contribute to a participant's reputation.

The contribution depends on the role performed within the agreement and the rules of the protocol.

Reputation is not treated as separate from the activity that generated it.

Each reputation signal is intended to remain connected to an underlying agreement and its record.

## Creator and Runner Reputation

RESXPECT recognises reputation generated through different roles.

### Runner Reputation

Reflects a history of completing work through agreements.

### Creator Reputation

Reflects a history of participating responsibly in agreements from the Creator side.

These roles can contribute to a broader reputation record while preserving the context in which the reputation was earned.

## Skill Reputation

Completed work can also contribute to reputation associated with particular skills.

Skill reputation is intended to demonstrate a history of work rather than simply a declared capability.

The strength of that signal may depend on factors such as the work performed, supporting evidence and the wider agreement history.

Detailed scoring mechanics are documented only where appropriate for the public protocol specification.

## Reputation Validation

Reputation may be subject to independent validation at defined points in the protocol.

Validators can review whether the reputation being generated is properly supported by:

- The agreement
- The work performed
- The skill being represented
- The evidence submitted

This helps protect the integrity of the reputation system.

## Reputation Integrity

RESXPECT includes mechanisms intended to reduce artificial or misleading reputation generation.

The public specification may describe the principles governing these protections.

Security-sensitive anti-abuse methods and internal detection systems are not disclosed where doing so could weaken their effectiveness.

## Public Specification

This repository documents the public rules and principles governing RESXPECT reputation.

Production implementation, private security systems and internal detection mechanisms remain outside the public repository.
