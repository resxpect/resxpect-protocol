# Skill Trust

Skill Trust represents the strength of evidence behind a participant's experience in a particular skill.

It is designed to answer a different question from general reputation:

**Has this person repeatedly demonstrated this skill through completed, reviewable work?**

## Purpose

A participant can declare that they have a skill.

Skill Trust is intended to show whether that claimed capability is supported by actual agreement history.

The principle is:

**Declared Skill → Completed Work → Evidence → Skill Trust**

## Skill-Specific History

Skill Trust is connected to the skill under which the work was completed.

This allows RESXPECT to distinguish between general agreement reputation and evidence of experience in a particular area.

For example, strong history in one skill does not automatically prove capability in another.

## Evidence

Evidence plays a central role in Skill Trust.

The strength of the skill signal can depend on the quality and relevance of the proof connected to completed work.

Different forms of work may therefore require different types of evidence.

## Agreement Context

Skill Trust is not generated from a skill declaration alone.

It is connected to qualifying agreements and the record behind them.

Factors such as the nature of the work, the agreement outcome and the supporting evidence may influence how strongly an agreement contributes to Skill Trust.

## Diversity of Experience

RESXPECT is designed to value broader experience over reputation generated from a narrow or repeatedly recycled set of relationships.

A stronger Skill Trust signal should therefore reflect evidence of work across meaningful agreement history rather than simple repetition.

## Validation

Skill Trust may be subject to Reputation Validation.

Validators can assess whether:

- The work genuinely relates to the skill being represented
- The evidence supports the completed work
- The evidence is suitable for supporting reputation

If the underlying agreement does not withstand validation, affected Skill Trust may be withheld or removed according to the protocol rules.

## Relationship to Reputation Points

**Reputation Points (RP)** reflect broader agreement history.

**Skill Trust** reflects evidence-backed experience connected to a particular skill.

The two systems are related, but they serve different purposes.

## Public Specification

This document describes the public principles behind Skill Trust.

Detailed scoring formulas, value limits, weighting systems, diversity multipliers, anti-farming thresholds and security-sensitive implementation are not disclosed in this repository.
