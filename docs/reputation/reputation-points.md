# Reputation Points

Reputation Points (RP) represent a participant's broader history of activity within RESXPECT.

RP is generated through agreement outcomes rather than self-declared claims, follower counts or standalone ratings.

## Purpose

RESXPECT uses RP to create a reputation record based on participation in real agreements.

The principle is:

**Actions within agreements → Outcomes → Reputation**

RP can increase or decrease depending on what happens during an agreement and how that outcome is validated by the protocol.

## Earning Reputation

Qualifying agreement outcomes may contribute positive RP.

The amount of reputation generated can depend on factors such as:

- The participant's role
- The agreement outcome
- The participant's existing reputation level
- Whether the activity qualifies under protocol reputation rules
- Validation where required

Not every interaction necessarily generates the same reputation effect.

## Losing Reputation

RP can also decrease when an agreement outcome demonstrates behaviour that should negatively affect a participant's reputation.

This allows RESXPECT reputation to represent both positive and negative agreement history rather than functioning only as an accumulation score.

## Creator and Runner Context

Reputation can be generated through participation on either side of an agreement.

### Runner

Runner reputation reflects the history of performing agreed work and reaching qualifying outcomes.

### Creator

Creator reputation reflects the history of participating responsibly from the agreement-creation and review side.

The protocol preserves the context in which reputation was generated.

## Reputation Levels

Accumulated RP contributes to a participant's reputation level.

Levels provide a simplified representation of a participant's wider agreement history without removing the underlying agreement record that produced it.

Progression is designed to become more meaningful as a participant builds a longer verified history.

## Repeated Activity

RESXPECT includes controls designed to prevent repeated activity between the same participants from being used to artificially generate reputation.

Repeated agreements may therefore contribute reduced or no additional RP where protocol conditions indicate that the reputation signal is no longer meaningfully independent.

## Reputation Validation

RP generated through agreements may be subject to Reputation Validation.

Where an agreement fails the required validation standard, reputation associated with affected activity may be withheld or removed according to the protocol rules.

This ensures that reputation remains connected to work and evidence that can withstand review.

## Reputation Is Not a Rating

RP is not intended to represent popularity.

It is a protocol record generated from agreement participation and outcomes.

A high reputation should therefore represent a deeper history of qualifying activity rather than simply a large number of positive reviews.

## Public Specification

This document describes the public principles governing Reputation Points.

Detailed anti-abuse detection methods, internal enforcement systems and security-sensitive implementation remain private.
